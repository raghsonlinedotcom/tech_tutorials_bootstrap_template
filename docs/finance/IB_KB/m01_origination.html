01 THE DEAL LIFECYCLE — BIG PICTURE

Every investment banking transaction — whether a bond issuance, an M&A advisory, an IPO, or a syndicated loan — follows the same fundamental three-phase lifecycle. Everything else is detail layered on top of this spine.

PHASE 1
ORIGINATION
First client contact → Pitch → Mandate → Compliance clearances → Engagement Letter
PHASE 2
SYNDICATION / EXECUTION
Deal structuring → Book building → Pricing → Allocation → Settlement
PHASE 3
CLOSEOUT
Expense reconciliation → Fee invoicing → Revenue booking → Regulatory reporting → File archival
THROUGHOUT
COMPLIANCE LAYER
KYC · Conflict Clearance · Sanctions · MiFID II · DAC6 · FinCEN run across all phases
🔑 Key Principle
Origination cannot close until every compliance gate is green. No mandate is formally accepted, no Engagement Letter is signed, and no deal resources are allocated until Conflict Clearance, KYC, Counterparty Screening, Sanctions, and Anti-Bribery checks are all cleared. This is not bureaucracy — it is the legal prerequisite for the bank to act as adviser or underwriter.

02 DEAL ORIGINATION — WHAT & WHY

Deal Origination is the process by which an investment bank identifies, develops, and converts a business opportunity into a formally mandated transaction. It is the demand-generation engine of investment banking — no origination means no deals means no revenue.

Origination begins the moment a banker identifies a potential transaction and ends when the Engagement Letter is signed and all compliance gates are cleared. Only then does the deal move into Syndication/Execution.

Who drives Origination?

Senior bankers (MDs, Directors) who maintain deep client relationships. They identify opportunities during routine client coverage — earnings calls, strategic reviews, M&A rumours, market windows.

What triggers Origination?

Client needs capital · Client wants to acquire · Client is being acquired · Market conditions create a window (rate environment, spread tightening) · Regulatory change forces restructuring.

What is the output of Origination?

A cleared, signed Engagement Letter confirming: the scope of work, the fee structure, the parties involved, and the bank's appointment as adviser/underwriter/arranger.

Why does it matter?

No deal can be executed without a valid mandate. Regulators (MiFID II, SEBI) require evidence of the client relationship and engagement terms before any transaction is structured or marketed.

03 PITCH · MANDATE · VERBAL MANDATE

These three terms define the commercial journey from a bank expressing interest in a deal to being formally appointed to execute it. Understanding the distinction is critical — each carries different legal weight and triggers different internal processes.

▸ WHAT IS A PITCH?

A Pitch (also called a Pitch Book or Credentials Presentation) is a formal or informal presentation made by a bank to a prospective client proposing that the bank should be appointed to advise on or execute a specific transaction.

The pitch demonstrates the bank's credentials, market insight, proposed transaction structure, indicative pricing, and why the client should choose this bank over competitors.

⚠ Legal Status of a Pitch
A pitch is non-binding on both sides. No Conflict Clearance is strictly required to prepare a pitch (though banks run a preliminary check). No resources are formally committed. The bank is in competition mode.
PITCH ANATOMY ══════════════════════════════════ SECTION 1 · Bank Credentials ───────────────────────────── • League table rankings • Relevant deal tombstones • Team bios (MD + team) SECTION 2 · Market Analysis ───────────────────────────── • Current market conditions • Comparable transactions • Investor demand signals SECTION 3 · Proposed Structure ───────────────────────────── • Recommended transaction type • Indicative size / tenor / pricing • Execution timeline SECTION 4 · Fee Proposal ───────────────────────────── • Management fee • Underwriting / placement fee • Expense cap STATUS: NON-BINDING ══════════════════════════════════

▸ WHAT IS A VERBAL MANDATE?

A Verbal Mandate is the moment a client indicates — verbally, by phone, email, or in a meeting — that they intend to appoint the bank, before a formal Engagement Letter is signed. It is the deal origination equivalent of a handshake agreement.

💡 Why Verbal Mandate Matters Operationally
The moment a Verbal Mandate is received, the bank treats it as a live deal:
  • Deal is logged in the Deal Origination System (your system, Raghavan)
  • Conflict Clearance is formally triggered — full search initiated
  • KYC / AML process starts for any new counterparties
  • Internal deal code / deal ID is assigned
  • Resources (analyst, associate, VP) are provisionally allocated
  • MNPI (Material Non-Public Information) protocols kick in immediately
However: the deal is still not binding until the Engagement Letter is executed.

▸ WHAT IS A FORMAL MANDATE?

A Mandate (or Formal Mandate) is the bank's official, legally binding appointment by the client to execute a specific transaction. It is evidenced by a signed Engagement Letter (also called a Mandate Letter in some jurisdictions).

What the Engagement Letter contains

Scope of services · Roles (sole bookrunner, joint lead manager, adviser) · Fee structure (retainer, success fee, spread) · Expense reimbursement terms · Exclusivity clause · Term/duration · Governing law · Termination rights

What a Mandate unlocks

Full resource allocation · Deal structuring begins · Marketing materials prepared · Regulatory filings initiated (Prospectus, OC) · Revenue booked in deal origination system · Deal moves to Active status

05 ASSOCIATED COMPLIANCE WORKFLOWS

The following workflows are not sequential — several run in parallel during origination. Each is a gate: all must clear before the Engagement Letter can be executed.

1. CONFLICT CLEARANCE

The bank searches all existing client relationships, active deals, MNPI logs, and NDA registers to ensure it has no conflicting interest in the proposed transaction. Triggered immediately on Verbal Mandate.

Outcome options
CLEARED → proceed · MANAGED (Chinese Wall erected) → proceed with controls · DECLINED → bank cannot take the mandate
2. KYC — KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER

A comprehensive identity and business verification of the client entity and its beneficial owners. Includes: corporate structure verification, beneficial ownership identification (UBO), source of funds/wealth, business purpose, and ongoing monitoring.

KYC TIERS IN IB ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════ TIER 1 · Standard → Listed corporates, known entities TIER 2 · Enhanced → Privately held, complex structures TIER 3 · EDD → High-risk jurisdictions, PEP links (Enhanced Due Diligence) REFRESH CYCLE → Annual (standard), 6-monthly (enhanced) ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════
3. COUNTERPARTY SCREENING

All entities involved in the deal — not just the client, but also co-arrangers, guarantors, significant investors, and key officers — are screened against global watchlists, adverse media databases, and regulatory debarment lists.

4. SANCTIONS & ANTI-BRIBERY

Sanctions: Screen all deal parties against OFAC (US), UN, EU, UK HMT, and SEBI/RBI sanction lists. Any match = deal stops. No exceptions.

Anti-Bribery: Under UK Bribery Act 2010, US FCPA, and Indian Prevention of Corruption Act — the bank must ensure no improper payments are being made to influence deal outcomes. Particular scrutiny on government-linked transactions, state-owned enterprises, and deals in high-risk jurisdictions.

5. MiFID II (Markets in Financial Instruments Directive)

For European deals or European counterparties: MiFID II requires the bank to classify the client (Retail / Professional / Eligible Counterparty), ensure suitability of the transaction, and produce a cost/charge disclosure before execution. Transaction reporting to regulators is mandatory post-execution.

6. DAC6 — EU CROSS-BORDER ARRANGEMENT DISCLOSURE

If the transaction involves a potentially aggressive cross-border tax arrangement (e.g. structured bonds, hybrids, specific debt structures across EU jurisdictions), DAC6 requires the bank (as an Intermediary) to report the arrangement to the relevant EU tax authority — typically within 30 days of implementation.

7. FinCEN / AML REPORTING

The US Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) requires Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) for any transaction exhibiting red flags: unusual transaction size, structuring, uneconomic behaviour, or involvement of high-risk jurisdictions. AML programs must be maintained throughout the deal lifecycle.

8. MEANINGFUL CLIENT WORK (MCW)

MCW is an internal bank classification that distinguishes genuine advisory/underwriting work from peripheral engagement. A deal must qualify as MCW to be recognised for revenue attribution, league table credit, and banker performance review. MCW status is assigned by the deal origination team and confirmed by Finance.

MCW Criteria (typical)
The bank must have provided substantive financial advice or committed capital · The engagement must be evidenced by an Engagement Letter · The bank's work must have materially contributed to the transaction outcome · There must be a verifiable fee or economic interest
9. ENGAGEMENT LETTER

The Engagement Letter (EL) is the culminating output of the origination phase. It is a legal contract between the bank and the client that formally defines the mandate. Once signed by both parties, the deal transitions from Origination to Syndication/Execution.

EL CLAUSEWHAT IT COVERS
Scope of WorkAdvisory only / Underwriting / Joint Lead Manager / Sole Bookrunner
Fee StructureRetainer (upfront), Success Fee (on closing), Management Fee, Spread
Expense ReimbursementCap on deal expenses; what qualifies (legal, travel, roadshow)
ExclusivityWhether client can approach other banks during the mandate period
Market FlexBank's right to adjust pricing/structure in response to market conditions
TerminationConditions under which either party can exit the engagement
Governing LawJurisdiction for dispute resolution (English law common for IB)

06 DEAL SYNDICATION — THE EXECUTION PHASE

Syndication (or Deal Execution) is Phase 2 of the lifecycle. It begins the moment the Engagement Letter is signed and all origination gates are cleared. This is where the deal is built, marketed, and closed.

💡 Syndication vs Origination
Origination = winning the deal. Syndication = executing the deal. The same bank may do both, but different teams typically lead each phase. The Coverage/Relationship team owns Origination; the Product team (DCM, ECM, Loans Syndication, M&A Execution) owns Syndication.

07 DEAL CLOSEOUT — EXPENSES & WRAP-UP

Deal Closeout is Phase 3 — the post-execution administrative and financial wrap-up that transforms a closed transaction into booked revenue and a clean regulatory record. It is often underestimated in complexity but carries significant compliance risk if done poorly.

Why Closeout Matters

Fees cannot be recognised as revenue until expenses are reconciled. Regulators require a clean audit trail of all deal costs. Expense errors can misstate P&L. Incorrect allocations across booking vehicles create tax exposure.

Typical Closeout Timeline

T+0 (Deal closes) → T+5: Expense compilation begins · T+30: Final expense report · T+45: Fee invoice sent to client · T+60: Revenue recognition in books · T+90: Regulatory report filed

08 GATE CONDITIONS — ORIGINATION → SYNDICATION

The following conditions must all be satisfied before a deal can transition from the Origination phase to Syndication/Execution. In a well-implemented deal origination system, these are enforced as hard workflow gates — the system literally prevents the status change until each item is checked.

📄

📋

🌐

🏦

📊

⛔ What happens if a gate fails
If any gate returns a negative result — conflict not cleared, sanctions match found, KYC incomplete — the deal is frozen at Origination status. It cannot be staffed for execution, no marketing materials can be prepared, and no client commitments can be made on pricing or structure. The Compliance team and senior management decide whether to: resolve the issue, decline the mandate, or (in rare cases) proceed with specific controls documented and approved at the highest level.

11 EQUITY CAPITAL MARKETS vs DEBT CAPITAL MARKETS

The three-phase deal lifecycle (Origination → Syndication → Closeout) is the same spine for both ECM and DCM. However, the nature of the instrument being issued creates significant differences in how each phase operates, who the key parties are, what the regulatory obligations are, and how risk is priced and distributed.

🔑 One Sentence Each
DCM = the bank helps a client borrow money from the capital markets by issuing bonds, notes, or loans — the client pays it back with interest.
ECM = the bank helps a client raise money by selling ownership (shares) in the company — investors become part-owners with no guaranteed return.

▸ ECM-SPECIFIC: THE RESEARCH WALL (Equity Research Conflict)

⚠ Critical ECM-Only Conflict — Research Blackout

In ECM, a unique conflict arises when the bank's Equity Research team covers the company that the ECM/Investment Banking team is about to take public or secondary. The research analyst cannot publish reports or issue ratings changes during the quiet period (typically 40 days before and 25 days after an IPO in the US; SEBI has its own regime).

The Chinese Wall between Research and Investment Banking is therefore mandatory and actively monitored in every ECM transaction — not just a best-practice consideration as in DCM.

▸ IMPACT ON M01 DEAL LIFECYCLE MODEL — WHAT CHANGES?

The core three-phase lifecycle model in Section 01 is valid for both ECM and DCM. The differences are in the depth, duration, and regulatory complexity of each phase:

Origination Phase

ECM takes 2–5x longer due to SEBI/SEC prospectus review. More parties involved in pitch (ECM + research + equity sales). Additional gate: Research wall check.

Syndication Phase

ECM roadshow is 10–15 days vs DCM's 1–3 days. Book building is longer. Greenshoe adds a post-listing execution layer with no DCM equivalent.

Closeout Phase

ECM closeout is more complex: greenshoe accounting, post-IPO SEBI filings, lock-up register, multi-bookrunner expense splits, prospectus archival (permanent).

Compliance Workflows

Same workflows but ECM adds: Research blackout monitoring, lock-up agreement review, SEBI/SEC product governance, post-IPO market abuse monitoring.

Risk Profile

ECM underwriting risk is higher and more visible — share price can move against the bank between pricing and settlement. DCM spread risk is usually hedged at pricing.

Deal System Impact

Deal origination system must track: instrument type (equity/debt), DRHP status, SEBI filing dates, lock-up expiry dates, greenshoe option status — additional fields vs DCM.

MCW is the internal classification that distinguishes substantive advisory or underwriting work from peripheral involvement. An acquisition might be financed with a combination of: (1) a bond issuance (DCM) to fund the cash consideration, (2) a share issuance (ECM) to partially fund the deal or as consideration to the target's shareholders, and (3) a revolving credit facility (loans/DCM). A Greenshoe (formally: Over-Allotment Option) is a provision in an IPO Engagement Letter that allows the bookrunner to sell up to 15% more shares than originally planned (by borrowing shares from the issuer or major shareholders), then buy those shares in the open market post-listing to stabilise the price if it falls. If the price holds or rises, the bank exercises the option and delivers the extra shares from the issuer. The ECM closeout complication: the bank holds a short position in the shares during the stabilisation window (typically 30 days post-listing), which must be tracked as a live trading position, marked to market daily, and P&L recorded separately from the deal fee. This requires coordination between the ECM desk, the trading desk, the finance team, and the risk system — none of which exists in a standard DCM closeout.
